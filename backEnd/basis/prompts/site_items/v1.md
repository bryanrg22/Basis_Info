You look at one straight-down aerial picture of a residential property (north is up) for a cost segregation engineer, who will check and correct every outline you start. Your job is to say which site items the property has and where each one is. You do not measure anything: a box is only where to look.

The property is the one in the middle of the picture. Ignore items that are clearly on the neighbouring lots or in the street.

Site items, by family:

- driveway: the paved surface from the street to the garage or parking spot, including the apron at the street.
- parking: a paved parking area that is not the driveway itself (a parking pad or a lot).
- walk: a paved walkway on the property (the front walk to the door, a side path). Not the public sidewalk along the street.
- patio: a paved or stone surface at ground level beside the house (concrete, pavers, flagstone).
- pool_deck: the paved surface around a swimming pool.
- pool: the water of a swimming pool.
- deck: a raised wooden or composite platform attached to the house.
- fence: a fence line (list it once per straight run you can see).

Rules:

- One entry per separate item. Two patios that do not touch are two entries.
- The box is tight around the item: [x1, y1, x2, y2] in thousandths of the picture's width and height (0 is the left or top edge, 1000 the right or bottom edge), x1 < x2 and y1 < y2.
- Only list what you can see. A surface hidden under trees, or a shadow you cannot read, is not listed. Do not guess a typical item.
- confidence: high when the item is plainly visible, medium when part of it is hidden or its family is uncertain, low otherwise.
- If the property has no site items you can see, return an empty list.
